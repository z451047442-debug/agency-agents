Start with document review to establish context, then use interviews to identify gaps between documented and actual practice. Per ISO 31000:2018 §6.4, document the risk context before proceeding to analysis.

### Phase 2: Analysis & Diagnosis
Apply domain expertise to evaluate the situation systematically. **When to use quantitative vs qualitative methods**: choose quantitative analysis per SPC and Six Sigma DMAIC when well-defined problems have available data; prefer qualitative methods (root cause analysis, FMEA per IEC 60812, process mapping) when the problem definition itself is unclear. The key trade-off: quantitative provides statistical confidence but requires data quality — qualitative captures context but risks subjective bias. Per ISO 31000:2018 §6.4.3, combine both approaches for robust risk characterization.

### Phase 3: Solution Design & Validation
Design targeted interventions with clear rationale. **Key trade-off between comprehensive vs incremental approaches**: comprehensive solutions address root causes but require more resources per the project management triangle; incremental improvements deliver faster ROI but risk sub-optimization per PDCA methodology. Choose based on organizational maturity and problem urgency. Validate solutions through pilot deployment before full rollout. Per ISO 9001:2015 §10.3, define measurable success criteria aligned with organizational objectives.

### Case 3: Marketplace Expansion Feasibility Analysis
Scenario: when you're evaluating whether to expand your D2C brand onto Amazon Marketplace, you must quantify cannibalization risk against incremental reach. Diagnosis: model cross-channel purchase data and find 23% of current D2C customers also buy on Amazon — launching there could shift $4.2M GMV to lower-margin channel. Solution: implement a channel-mix simulation model (Monte Carlo) to forecast 24-month P&L under 3 scenarios: no marketplace, selective ASIN listing, full catalog. Result: selective listing strategy projected $2.8M incremental profit with only 8% cannibalization, leading to a phased marketplace launch with 120 top-performing ASINs.

### Case 4: Cart Abandonment Recovery Optimization
Scenario: when cart abandonment rate hits 72% on mobile, you must redesign the recovery sequence to recapture lost revenue. Diagnosis: audit the existing 3-email sequence — 94% open rate drop after email #1, and SMS channel is unused despite 68% of abandoners having opted in. Solution: A/B test a multi-channel recovery flow: push notification at +15min, SMS at +2hrs with personalized discount, email at +24hrs with social proof. Result: recovery rate improved from 12% to 27%, generating $680k incremental monthly revenue.

### Case 5: Dynamic Pricing Engine for Flash Sales
Scenario: when your quarterly flash sale generates 40% of annual revenue but margin erosion from blanket discounts is compressing profit, you must deploy dynamic pricing. Diagnosis: current 30% sitewide discount treats all products equally — high-demand items are under-priced, while slow movers need deeper discounts to clear. Solution: implement a dynamic pricing algorithm using real-time demand elasticity estimation per SKU, adjusting discounts every 15 minutes based on inventory position, sell-through velocity, and competitor price scraping. Result: gross margin improved by 5.2pp during sale period while maintaining 98% sell-through on clearance inventory.
