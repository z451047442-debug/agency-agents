### Case 1: Seasonal Markdown Optimization
Scenario: when you're managing a women's ready-to-wear brand with 45% of annual revenue concentrated in Q4 (holiday) and end-of-season markdowns eroding 12% of gross margin annually, you must reduce markdown depth without increasing terminal inventory. Diagnosis: sell-through analysis using Retail Pro or Aptos POS data reveals markdowns are applied uniformly across all stores at 6-week intervals, but store-level sell-through velocity varies 4x between flagship locations (selling at full price) and suburban stores (needing markdowns at week 3). The OTB (Open-to-Buy) plan is set at the category level without size-level granularity, causing stockouts in best-selling sizes (8-10) while size 2-4 accumulate excess. Solution: implement size-level inventory allocation using AI demand forecasting (Celect or Nextail) that redistributes replenishment based on store-level sell-through, introduce dynamic markdown optimization with price elasticity modeling per SKU/store combination (using PredictSpring or DynamicAction), and restructure the buying calendar to shift 15% of Q4 open-to-buy into pre-season test-and-reorder programs (small initial buys with 4-week reorder capability from in-season manufacturing). Result: markdown depth reduced from 45% to 32% average, inventory turnover improved from 3.2x to 4.8x annually, gross margin improved by 5 percentage points while sell-through rate increased from 62% to 74%.

### Case 2: DTC Channel Launch for Wholesale-Dependent Brand
Scenario: you run a contemporary fashion brand generating 78% of $45M revenue through department store wholesale (Nordstrom, Saks, Bloomingdale's) — but wholesale margins are 38% and department store traffic is declining 6% annually. You must build a DTC e-commerce channel without alienating wholesale partners. Diagnosis: launching a full-price DTC site would trigger wholesale channel conflict (retail partners require price parity and may reduce orders if they perceive brand as competitor). The brand has no customer data — all end-consumer data sits with retail partners. Solution: launch DTC on Shopify Plus with a differentiated assortment strategy — DTC carries exclusive capsule collections, extended sizes, and pre-order access to runway pieces that wholesale partners don't stock. Implement customer data capture via Klaviyo email/SMS pop-ups and loyalty program (Sweetbridge or Yotpo) to build a first-party data asset. For pricing, maintain full MSRP on DTC while wholesale partners run their own markdown cadence — DTC serves as the "brand home" that sets the aspirational price point. Fulfill through a 3PL (ShipBob or Ryder) with branded packaging and handwritten thank-you notes for orders above $200. Track wholesale partner sentiment via quarterly business reviews and monitor order volume changes — if any partner reduces orders by more than 15%, trigger a joint marketing campaign funded at 2% of DTC revenue. Result: DTC reached $7.2M in year 2 at 72% gross margin (vs. 38% wholesale), cumulative customer database grew to 85,000 profiles, wholesale revenue remained stable at $43M (partners did not reduce orders because DTC assortment was differentiated), total company revenue grew 22% with blended margin improving from 38% to 48%.

### Case 3: Supply Chain Agility — Near-Shoring Strategy
Scenario: your fast-fashion brand sources 92% of production from Southeast Asia (Vietnam, Bangladesh, Cambodia) with 90-day lead times, but fast-fashion competitors using near-shore production (Turkey, Portugal, Morocco) achieve 21-day lead times — your Zara-style weekly drop model is impossible with current supply chain geography. Diagnosis: the cost differential is real — Bangladesh CMT (cut-make-trim) costs $4.50/unit vs. Turkey at $9.80/unit, but the hidden costs of long lead times include: air freight surcharges ($4.20/unit when 30% of orders are expedited to meet in-season demand), markdowns on late deliveries (9% of orders arrive after the optimal selling window), and lost sales from stockouts on trending items. Solution: implement a dual-supply-chain strategy: 60% of volume remains in Asia for core/never-out-of-stock styles (basic tees, denim, underwear where trend risk is minimal) using sea freight at $0.40/unit, while 40% shifts to near-shore production (Turkey for European distribution, Mexico for North America) for trend-responsive styles with 21-day lead times. Invest in PLM integration (Centric PLM with supplier portal) to share tech packs and BOM data digitally with near-shore factories, reducing sample iteration cycles from 3 weeks to 5 days. Renegotiate Asia factory MOQs (minimum order quantities) from 3,000 units per style/color to 1,000 units in exchange for 3-year volume commitment. Result: lead time for trend items reduced from 90 to 21 days, air freight costs decreased by 72%, markdown rate improved from 38% to 27%, total landed cost per unit increased only 6% (offset by freight savings and higher full-price sell-through), and in-season replenishment capability enabled capturing 3 additional trend cycles per year.
