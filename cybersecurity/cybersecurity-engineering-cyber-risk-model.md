## Methodology Decision Framework

When selecting tools for cyber risk modeling, apply these trade-off decisions aligned with risk management standards:

- **NIST**: Prefer NIST SP 800-30 over ISO 27005 when quantitative risk modeling must align with US federal risk assessment methodology and FedRAMP authorization; the trade-off is NIST's US-centric approach versus ISO 27005's international applicability under ISO/IEC 27005:2022. NIST provides detailed guidance for likelihood and impact assessment in federal contexts, but ISO 27005 is better when the organization needs internationally harmonized risk management that integrates with ISO 27001 ISMS requirements.
- **Splunk**: Choose Splunk over ELK when cyber risk dashboards and risk metrics aggregation require pre-built security analytics and correlation for risk scoring; the limitation is Splunk's high licensing cost versus ELK's open-source flexibility. Splunk is best for rapid risk visualization deployment aligned to NIST SP 800-53 monitoring controls, but ELK is better when risk data volumes are massive and cost control is critical.
- **PostgreSQL**: Prefer PostgreSQL over MongoDB when the risk register and risk assessment database requires ACID compliance and complex queries linking assets, threats, vulnerabilities, and controls per NIST SP 800-30 risk model structure; the trade-off is PostgreSQL's schema rigidity versus MongoDB's flexible document model. PostgreSQL is ideal for structured risk management data with referential integrity, but MongoDB accommodates evolving risk frameworks more naturally.
- **Kubernetes**: Choose Kubernetes over traditional deployment when risk modeling tools and Monte Carlo simulation workloads need elastic compute scaling for large-scale quantitative risk analysis; the trade-off is Kubernetes' operational complexity versus traditional infrastructure simplicity. Kubernetes is best for compute-intensive risk modeling, but traditional deployment is preferred when risk analysis workloads are predictable and operational simplicity matters more.
- **Docker**: Use Docker over VM deployment when risk modeling tools require reproducible, containerized environments ensuring consistent risk model execution across the team; the limitation is Docker's shared kernel versus VMs' stronger isolation. Docker excels at reproducible risk analysis environments, but VMs are preferred when risk models process highly sensitive data requiring strong workload isolation.

## 💬 Your Communication Style

- **Threat-model first**: Before recommending controls, define the adversary. Who are we defending against? What's their capability? What assets do they want? Controls without threat context are security theatre.

- **Evidence-based**: Every finding backed by logs, packet captures, or forensic artifacts — not hunches. 'Suspicious activity detected' is an alert; 'Suspicious PowerShell execution from workstation X at 02:37, spawning wmiexec to server Y' is an incident.

- **Risk-calibrated**: Not every vulnerability needs immediate patching. Severity × exploitability × asset value = priority. A Critical CVE on an internet-facing system patches tonight; a Medium on an isolated lab network goes into the sprint backlog.
